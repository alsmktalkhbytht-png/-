               Introduction
• Microbiological identification determines the
  type, genus, or species of microorganisms.
• It is essential for disease diagnosis,
  antimicrobial selection, food safety, and
  microbial research.
• Combines classical (phenotypic) and modern
  (molecular) methods for accuracy.
 Objectives of Microbial Identification


1- Determine etiologic agents of infection
2- Understand microbial diversity and taxonomy
3- Guide therapy and infection control
4- Trace outbreaks and perform surveillance
 General Steps in Identification
• 1. Sample Collection – Aseptic techniques to avoid
  contamination
• 2. Microscopic Examination – Morphology and
  staining
• 3. Cultivation – Selective and differential media
• 4. Biochemical Characterization – Metabolic profiling
• 5. Serological Testing – Antigen or antibody detection
• 6. Molecular Identification – DNA/RNA based
  methods
Classical (Phenotypic) Methods: Microscopic
                Examination
• - Gram Stain: Differentiates Gram-positive
  (purple) and Gram-negative (pink)
• - Acid-Fast Stain: Detects Mycobacterium
• - Special Stains: Endospore, Capsule, and
  Flagella for structure identification
        Cultural Characteristics
• - Colony morphology: size, color, texture,
  elevation
• - Growth conditions: aerobic, anaerobic,
  microaerophilic
• - Hemolysis patterns on blood agar: α, β, γ
• - Pigment and odor production
       Biochemical Identification
• Biochemical tests determine bacterial metabolism:
 Catalase: EX Staphylococcus (+) vs. Streptococcus (–)
• - Oxidase:EX Pseudomonas (+), Neisseria (+)
• - Indole: EX E. coli (+)
• - Urease: Proteus (+)
• - Citrate:EX Enterobacter (+)
• - TSI Agar: EX Sugar fermentation and H₂S production
• - Coagulase:EX S. aureus (+)

      Serological Identification
• - Agglutination tests: Salmonella, Shigella
  typing
• - Latex agglutination: Streptococcus grouping
• - ELISA: Detects bacterial toxins/antigens
• - Western Blot: Confirms microbial proteins
• Advantages: Rapid, specific, sensitive.
Molecular Identification Methods
• - PCR: Amplifies specific DNA sequences
• - DNA Hybridization: Uses complementary
  DNA probes
• - Microarrays: Identify multiple pathogens
  simultaneously
Automated Identification Systems
• Modern systems for rapid identification:
• - VITEK 2
• - BD Phoenix
• - MicroScan WalkAway
• - MALDI-TOF MS
• Combine accuracy, speed, and antimicrobial
  susceptibility testing.
Antimicrobial Susceptibility Testing (AST)

• - Kirby-Bauer Disk Diffusion
• - Minimum Inhibitory Concentration (MIC)
• - E-test strips
• - Automated systems (VITEK, Phoenix)
• Determine resistance profiles for treatment
  decisions.
     Advantages and Limitations
• Method       | Advantages | Limitations
• Microscopy | Rapid, simple | Low specificity
• Culture       | Reliable    | Time-consuming
• Biochemical | Cost-effective | May misidentify
  atypical strains
• Serological | Specific       | Requires known
  antigens
• Molecular | Fast, accurate | Expensive, needs
  expertise
                Conclusion
• Microbiological identification integrates
  classical and molecular methods.
• Combining phenotypic, biochemical,
  serological, and molecular techniques ensures
  accurate and fast diagnostics.
• Automation and sequencing are the future of
  microbial identification.
