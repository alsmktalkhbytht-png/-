Serum Amylase
Amylase is an enzyme that catalyses the hydrolysis of starch into sugars. This
enzyme produced by:
1) Salivary glands.
2) Pancreas.
3) Liver.
• Much lower activities are present in the ovaries, intestines, and skeletal muscle.
• If there is an inflammation of the above (pancreas or salivary glands) much
amylase enters the blood.
Types of amylase:
1) α- amylase " animal and human tissue " .
2) ᵝ- amylase “plant and bacterial organism".
Normal value:
The normal range is 40 to 140 units per liter (U/L)
Clinical significance:
1) Acute pancreatitis
2) Carcinoma of head of the pancreas
3) Peptic ulcers.
4) Salivary gland disorder
5) Tumor of salivary glands
6) Intestinal obstruction and etc
Somogyi unit: it’s a unit of amylase activity defined as the amount of enzyme that
is able to liberate sugars with a reducing value equivalent to 1 gm of glucose in the
course of a 30 min reaction at 37C and PH =7.

Serum Lipase
Lipase is an enzyme the body uses to break down fats in food into fatty acids and
glycerol so they can be absorbed in the intestines.
Lipase is produced in the pancreas, mouth, and stomach. Most people produce
enough pancreatic lipase, but people with cystic fibrosis, Crohn disease, and celiac
disease may not have enough lipase to get the nutrition they need from food.
Lipase appears in the blood following pancreatic damage at the same time amylase
appears (or slightly later) but remains elevated much longer than amylase (7 to 10
days).
Amylase and lipase tests are used to diagnose and monitor treatment of acute
pancreatitis and to differentiate pancreatitis from other acute abdominal disorders.
- 80% of patients with acute pancreatitis will have elevated amylase and
lipase levels but lipase remains elevated longer than amylase.
- Lipase assay provides better sensitivity and specificity and is best used with
amylase determination.
Normal value:
- Adults: 10–140 U/L
- Elderly persons (>60 years): 18–180 U/L
The principle:
Pancreatic lipase catalyses the hydrolysis of Triolein to form monoglycerides and
fatty acids.
The sample: must be serum free form hemolysis.
