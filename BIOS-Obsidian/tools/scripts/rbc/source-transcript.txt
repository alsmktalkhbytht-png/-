Introduction and principle

Red blood cells make up almost 45 % of the blood volume.

Each RBC is a biconcave disc having a diameter of 7.2 microns and a thickness of 2.2 microns.

Red blood cells are so packed with hemoglobin that they lack many components, including a nucleus, found in other cells.

Their primary function is to carry oxygen from the lungs to every cell in the body.

RED BLOOD CELL COUNT

The red blood cell count is the number of red blood cells per unit volume of whole blood.

RBC count is down to determine whether there is an adequate number of RBC in the circulation or not.

The Average lifespan of Red Blood Cells RBCs) is 100 – 120 days.

Manual Method

Materials and instruments

1- Anticoagulated whole (using EDTA or heparin as an anticoagulant) or capillary blood can be used.

2- HAYEM'S solution ( diluting fluid) which is composed of :

*HgCl2 0.05 g

*Na2SO4 2.5 g

* NaCl 0.5 g

* distilled water 100 ml

3- RBC pipette which is composed of stem and mixing chamber with red bead it is function is to mix blood with the substance and for differentiation form the WBC pipette

[Slide 7]

4-Haemocytometer ( Neubeuer) chamber is counting chamber with a cover slip . the same counting chamber is used also for counting total white blood cells

5- Microscope

6- lancet

7- alcohol 70%

8- cotton

[Slide 8]

Procedure

1- Wipe your partner's finger with cotton soaked with alcohol and allow it to dry .

2-With a sterile disposable lancet do small prick on the finger tip , when a drop of reasonable size has collected

3- Hold the red blood cell pipette slightly tilted from the vertical position ; and apply it's tip to the drop and aspirate blood to the mark 0.5 .

4-Wipe off any blood adhering to its outer side ; if the blood gets 0.5 marks tap the tip gently till the blood is exactly at the mark. never allow the blood to clot inside the pipette ; if the blood clots in the pipette blow the sample out, clean the pipette and begin all over again .

5-Aspirate diluting Hayem's solution to the 101 mark thus making 1:200 dilution of blood.

[Slide 9]

6- Hold the pipette horizontally and role it with both hands between finger and thumb

7-Brow out a quarter of the contents to remove the pure diluting fluid in the stem.

8-Prepare the counting chamber and cover slip , then hold the pipette in 45⁰ and touch it tip gently on the surface of the counting platform where it projects beyond the cover slip and a small amount of solution will be drawn under the cover slip

9-Place the Neubauer chamber on the stage of the microscope and allow 2 minutes for the cells to settle .

[Slide 10]

10-Scan the counting area with 10 X objective lens.

11- Use the 40X objective include all cells lying on the lower and right line of any square omit the cells on the upper and left hand lines

12- Count the cells in 5 medium squares of 16 small square i.e. 80 small squares one at each corner and one in the center

Calculation

Count the number (N) of cells on 80 small squares located in 5 middle sizes squares ( four located at the four corner and one in middle ) in which (N) number of cells in 1 mm 3are found is :

N x 10000 = TOTAL RBCs

Normal values

Normal red blood cells values at various ages are

Newborn: 4.8-7.2 million

Adults (males): 4.9-5.5 million

(Females): 4.4-5.0 million

Pregnancy: slightly lower than normal adult values

Children: 3.8-5.5 million

Medical conditions

Polycythemia is a disease of unknown origin that results in an abnormal increase in red blood cells.

Anemia: is a general term that refers to a decrease in red blood cells.

Anemia can occur from either a decrease in the number of red blood cells, a decrease in the hemoglobin content, or both.

A lower-than-normal RBC can result from a number of causes, including:

Massive RBC loss, such as acute hemorrhage

Abnormal destruction of RBC

Lack of substances needed for RBC production

Chemotherapy or radiation side effects from treatment of bone marrow malignancies such as leukemia can result in bone marrow suppression.

NORMAL PHYSIOLOGICAL CONDITIONS

Males have RBC count more than females due to many factors like the male hormones ( androgen) and also due to large muscle mass in males that need more O2 , another factor is that the female losses an amount of blood during the menstrual cycle .

A normal physiological increase in the RBC count occurs at high places or after intense exercise.

smokers have a higher number of RBCs than non-smokers
