Phylum Apicomplexa; Main properties of the group; Ultrastructure of the
apical complex

Apicomplexa are characterized by their distinctive apical complex for host
cell invasion, a relict non- photosynthetic plastid called an apicoplast for
survival, complex life cycles involving sexual and asexual reproduction, and
gliding motility for movement.
Most are obligate parasites of animals, lack external locomotion like flagella
or pseudopods, and include well- known pathogens like Plasmodium Malaria
and Toxoplasma

Key properties

Apical complex: A specialized structure at the cell's anterior end that
includes secretory organelles (micronemes, rhoptries) and cytoskeletal
elements, which secrete enzymes needed to penetrate and invade host cells

Apicoplast: A relict, non- photosynthetic organelle derived from a red alga
via secondary endosymbiosis, it is essential for the apicomplexan's survival

Gliding motility: Apicomplexan use a unique form of locomotion called
gliding motility to move across surfaces and locate hosts, as they lack flagella
or pseudopods in their active stages

Parasitism

Most apicomplexans are obligate endoparasites of animals, meaning they
must live inside a host to survive.

Complex life cycles

They often have intricate life cycles with different stages of asexual (e.g.,
schizogony) and sexual reproduction (gametogony), as well as spore
formation (Sporogony).

Microscopic structure Apicomplexans are unicellular, eukaryotic organisms

General characters of Apicomplexa

The Apicomplexa are a monophyletic group composed almost entirely of
parasitic ( i. e. no free – living) species.
The parasite belonging to this group of protozoa don’t possess any type of
locomotion organelles, they show slight amoeboid change of form (body
flexion).
Electron microscopy revealed unique ultrastructural features among the
various sporozoan which were subsequently used to redefine the groups. A
defining characteristic of the Apicomplexa is a group of organelles found at
one end – called the apical end – of the organism.
This apical complex includes secretory organelles known as micronemes and
rhoptries, polar ring composed of microtubules, and in some species a conoid
which lies within the polar rings.
The Apicomplexa have complex life cycle that are characterized by three
distinct processes, sporogony, merogony and gametogony (alternation of
generation). May be classified into two processes schizogony and
gametogony
Sporogony is an asexual reproduction that culminates in the production of
sporozoites. Sporozoites will develop into forms that undergo another asexual
replication known as merogony. Merogony and the resulting merozoites are
known by many different names depending on the species.
Similarly, sporogony and gametogony can involve different hosts or cell
types. As an alternative to asexual replication merozoites can become
gametes through a process variously called gametogony, or gametogenesis.
As in other types of sexual reproduction, the gametes fuse to form a zygote
which differentiates into a form yielding sporozoites.
