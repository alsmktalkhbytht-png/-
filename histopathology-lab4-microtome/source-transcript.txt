What is Microtomy?
• Microtomy is the technique of preparing extremely thin sections of
biological tissues, plants, or materials using a specialized instrument called
a microtome. These thin sections, typically only a few micrometers thick (1
µm = 1/1000 of a millimeter), are then mounted on glass slides and stained
to be examined under a light or electron microscope.
• The primary goal of microtomy is to allow light or electrons to pass through
the specimen, making its internal structures visible and enabling detailed
histological, pathological, or cytological analysis.

A microtome is a precision cutting tool. While designs vary, all
microtomes consist of a few key components:
1. Base & Clamp: A heavy, stable base to prevent vibration, and a clamp to hold the
specimen block firmly in place.
2. Knife Holder: A mount for the cutting blade (steel, glass, or diamond).
3. Specimen Feed Mechanism: An extremely precise mechanism (manual or automatic)
that advances the specimen block a specific distance (e.g., 4 µm, 5 µm) towards the
knife after each cutting stroke. This determines the thickness of the section.
4. Cutting Window/Arm: The part that the user moves to perform the cutting stroke.

Types of Microtomes
• Different microtomes are used for different types of specimens and
embedding media:
1. Rotary Microtome: The most common type in histology and
pathology labs.
• How it works: The specimen block is moved vertically past a stationary knife
in a rotary motion of a handwheel.
• Best for: Producing large numbers of thin, serial sections (2-10 µm) from
paraffin-embedded tissues.

Rotary Microtome

2- Cryostat (Freezing Microtome):
• How it works: A rotary microtome housed in a refrigerated
chamber (typically -15°C to -30°C).
• Best for: Cutting frozen tissue sections. Tissues are frozen and
embedded in a freezing medium, not paraffin. This is essential
for:
1. Rapid intra-operative diagnosis (e.g., during surgery to determine
if a tumor is cancerous).
2. Demonstrating lipids or enzymes that would be destroyed by hot
paraffin processing.
3. Immunohistochemistry on sensitive antigens.

Cryostat (Freezing Microtome):

3- Sledge Microtome:
• How it works: The specimen block is mounted on a
"sledge" that slides past a long, stationary knife.
• Best for: Cutting very large blocks or hard materials,
including celloidin-embedded tissues and some rigid
plastics.

Sledge Microtome:

4- Ultramicrotome:
• How it works: An extremely precise microtome that uses a glass or
diamond knife to cut sections in a fluid-filled trough (a "boat").
• Best for: Preparing ultra-thin sections (50-100 nanometers)
for Transmission Electron Microscopy (TEM). The sections are so
thin they must be collected on small copper grids.

Ultramicrotome:

5- Vibratome (Vibrating Microtome):
• How it works: Uses a vibrating blade to cut through tissue. The tissue is not
frozen or embedded in paraffin; it is simply submerged in a buffer solution.
• Best for: Cutting fresh, unfixed, or lightly fixed tissues. It preserves cell
viability and antigenicity better than other methods but produces thicker
sections (30-500 µm).

Vibratome (Vibrating Microtome):

1- The block is secured in the microtome.
2- A sharp microtome knife or disposable blade is installed.

3- The thickness is set (e.g., 4-5 µm).

4- The user rotates the handwheel in a
smooth, rhythmic motion.

5- As sections are cut, the static electricity causes them to adhere
to each other, forming a continuous "ribbon" of serial sections.

Float-Out Bath:
• The ribbon is carefully manipulated with a brush or forceps and
floated on a warm water bath (usually 5-10°C below the melting point
of the paraffin).
• The warmth relaxes the sections, removing wrinkles and folds.

• Mounting:
1- A glass microscope slide is submerged under the floating
section.

2- The section is carefully "fished" out onto the slide and
positioned.

3- The slide is drained and dried in an oven to firmly
adhere the section to the glass.

Staining: The slide is then processed through stains (like
Hematoxylin and Eosin - H&E) to visualize cellular components.
