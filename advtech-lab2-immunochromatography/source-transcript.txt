What Is Immunochromatography?

Immunochromatography, also known as lateral flow immunoassay (LFIA) or simply a rapid test, is a rapid diagnostic technique used to detect the presence or absence of a target analyte (such as a specific antigen or antibody) in a liquid sample without the need for specialized laboratory equipment.

IMMUNO

Specific antigen–antibody recognition

CHROMATOGRAPHY

Movement through a membrane by capillary flow

Together → a visible or reader-detected signal at the test region.

[1] Koczula & Gallotta, Essays Biochem. 2016; [2] Boehringer & O'Farrell, Clin Chem. 2022

The sample moves along the strip.

Anatomy of a Lateral-Flow Strip

Sample Pad

Conjugate Pad

Nitrocellulose Membrane

Absorbent Pad

Sample pad: receives and conditions the specimen.

Conjugate pad: stores dried labeled antibodies or antigens.

Nitrocellulose membrane: contains the Test (T) and Control (C) capture regions.

Absorbent pad: pulls fluid through the strip and collects excess liquid.

Sample Pad & Conjugate Pad

Sample Pad

• Receives the specimen

• Helps distribute fluid evenly

• May filter or condition the sample

• Begins capillary migration

Conjugate Pad

• Contains dried labeled detection reagents

• Labels may include colloidal gold or colored/fluorescent particles

• Rehydrates when the sample reaches it

• Target + labeled reagent form a migrating complex

Practical point: incorrect specimen volume or poor sample preparation can disturb flow before the sample ever reaches the test line.

[2] Boehringer & O'Farrell, 2022

Test Line, Control Line & Absorbent Pad

Test Line (T)

Captures the target and produces the test signal.

Control Line (C)

Shows that the test worked correctly.

No control line = Invalid result.

Absorbent Pad

Helps the sample keep moving and collects excess liquid.

What About a Faint Test Line?

A faint line may still represent a reactive/positive result in many qualitative tests — but only if the manufacturer defines it that way and it appears within the permitted reading time.

Do not compare line darkness between different brands unless the kit supports that interpretation.

Do not read the cassette after the stated time window.

Repeat or confirm according to the test algorithm when the result is uncertain.

0 ng/mL 1 ng/mL 2 ng/mL

Interpretation is kit-specific; follow manufacturer IFU and local testing algorithm

Common Causes of False or Invalid Results

Pre-analytical / User Errors

• Wrong specimen type

• Poor specimen collection

• Incorrect sample volume

• Wrong buffer volume

• Expired or improperly stored kit

• Cross-contamination

Analytical / Interpretation Errors

• Incorrect timing

• Cassette placed on an unsuitable surface

• Failure of control line

• Misreading faint lines

• Reading after the allowed time

• Ignoring kit-specific interpretation rules

WHO notes that incorrect performance or interpretation can compromise RDT utility [3]
