Red Blood Cell Indices
MCV  •  MCH  •  MCHC  •  RDW
Presented by: Linda Munjed Hadi
Introduction and Principle
Anemia is a reduction in hemoglobin concentration or red blood cell mass that reduces the oxygen-carrying capacity of blood.
Red blood cell indices describe cell size, hemoglobin amount, hemoglobin concentration, and variation in cell size.
These indices help classify anemia and guide further investigations.
Four Important Red Blood Cell Indices
Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV): average red blood cell size.
Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin (MCH): average amount of hemoglobin per cell.
Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration (MCHC): hemoglobin concentration in packed red cells.
Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW): variation in red cell size.
Mean Corpuscular Volume (MCV)
Definition: the average volume of one red blood cell.
Formula: MCV (fL) = Hematocrit (%) × 10 ÷ RBC count (million/µL).
Typical adult reference interval: 80–100 fL.
Microcytic: <80 fL  •  Normocytic: 80–100 fL  •  Macrocytic: >100 fL.
fL = femtoliter (10⁻¹⁵ liter).
MCV: Calculation Example
Given: Hematocrit = 45%; RBC count = 5.5 million/µL.
MCV = (45 × 10) ÷ 5.5
MCV = 81.8 fL.
Interpretation: normocytic red blood cells.
Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin (MCH)
Definition: average amount of hemoglobin in a single red blood cell.
Formula: MCH (pg) = Hemoglobin (g/dL) × 10 ÷ RBC count (million/µL).
Typical adult reference interval: 27–33 pg.
pg = picogram (10⁻¹² gram).
Low MCH indicates reduced hemoglobin amount per cell.
MCH: Calculation Example
Given: Hemoglobin = 12.5 g/dL; RBC count = 4.1 million/µL.
MCH = (12.5 × 10) ÷ 4.1
MCH = 30.5 pg.
Interpretation: within the typical adult reference interval.
Mean Corpuscular Hemoglobin Concentration (MCHC)
Definition: average concentration of hemoglobin in packed red blood cells.
Formula: MCHC (g/dL) = Hemoglobin (g/dL) × 100 ÷ Hematocrit (%).
Typical adult reference interval: 32–36 g/dL.
Low MCHC suggests hypochromia.
High MCHC can occur in hereditary spherocytosis or from analytical interference.
MCHC: Calculation Example
Given: Hemoglobin = 12.5 g/dL; Hematocrit = 37%.
MCHC = (12.5 × 100) ÷ 37
MCHC = 33.8 g/dL (approximately 33.7 if truncated).
Interpretation: within the typical adult reference interval.
MCH versus MCHC
MCH measures the amount of hemoglobin in one red blood cell (pg).
MCHC measures the concentration of hemoglobin within packed red blood cells (g/dL).
Cells can have a low MCH because they are small, even when MCHC is near normal.
Use both indices with MCV and the blood smear.
Red Cell Distribution Width (RDW)
Definition: a measure of variation in red blood cell size (anisocytosis).
Typical RDW-CV reference interval: approximately 11.5–14.5%.
High RDW indicates greater variation in cell size.
Normal RDW indicates relatively uniform cell sizes.
RDW is most useful when interpreted together with MCV.
Medical Application: Microcytic Anemia
Microcytic anemia: small red blood cells, usually MCV <80 fL in adults.
Cells are often hypochromic, with increased central pallor.
Iron deficiency is a common cause; thalassemia is another important cause.
Defective hemoglobin production may involve iron metabolism, heme synthesis, or globin synthesis.
RDW is often high in iron deficiency, but patterns vary.
Medical Application: Normocytic Anemia
Normocytic anemia: anemia with a typical adult MCV of 80–100 fL.
Possible causes include acute blood loss, hemolysis, kidney disease, and some chronic inflammatory diseases.
Normal MCV does not exclude mixed red blood cell populations.
Reticulocyte count, blood smear, and clinical history help interpretation.
Medical Application: Macrocytic Anemia
Macrocytosis in adults: MCV >100 fL.
Megaloblastic causes: vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, with impaired DNA synthesis.
Non-megaloblastic causes include liver disease, alcohol use, and hypothyroidism.
Myelodysplastic syndromes can also cause macrocytosis.
Blood smear findings and further tests are required to determine the cause.
Hypochromia and Its Causes
Hypochromia means red blood cells appear paler than normal on a stained blood smear.
It is often associated with reduced hemoglobin concentration and increased central pallor.
Possible causes include iron deficiency from inadequate intake, poor absorption, or blood loss.
Other causes include thalassemia, some chronic diseases, and lead toxicity.
Low MCHC supports hypochromia, but blood smear assessment remains important.
Classification of Anemia Using Indices
Microcytic, often hypochromic: low MCV; MCH often low; MCHC may be low.
Normocytic, normochromic: MCV and MCHC within reference intervals.
Macrocytic: high MCV; MCH may be increased because cells are larger.
RDW helps detect variation in red cell sizes; it is not uniformly high in every anemia type.
Summary
MCV → average cell size (fL).
MCH → hemoglobin amount per cell (pg).
MCHC → hemoglobin concentration (g/dL).
RDW → variation in red blood cell size (%).
Interpret indices with hemoglobin, hematocrit, RBC count, and blood smear.
