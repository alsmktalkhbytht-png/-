# Introduction of Enzymes

They are biological catalysts which bring about chemical reaction in the living cell.

**Functions:** digestion, breathing, synthesis and break down of CHOS, proteins, fats.

## General properties of enzymes

1. All enzymes are proteins.
2. Enzymes accelerate the reaction but:
   - do not alter the reaction equilibrium.
   - not consumed in overall reaction.
   - required in very small quantities.
3. Enzymes are highly specific for their substrate.
4. Enzymes possess active site, at which interaction with substrate take place.

## Sources of enzymes

1. Enzymes that function within the cells, most of enzymes are these types:
   - Endoenzymes. Example: metabolic oxidase.
2. Enzymes that are liberated by cells and catalyze reactions outside the cell:
   - Exoenzymes. Example: digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase, protease).

## Chemical composition of enzymes

1. Enzyme consists of only protein.

   Example: pepsin, trypsin (amino acids binding peptide bonds).

## Definitions

- **Coenzyme:** are typically organic molecules, used by enzymes to help catalyse reactions, contain functionalities not found in proteins.
- **Cofactors:** are catalytically essential molecules or ions that are covalently bound to the enzyme.
- **Holoenzyme:** enzyme consist of Apoenzyme + prosthetic group.
- **Apoenzyme:** term refers to the protein part of enzyme.
- **Active site of enzyme:** the point in the enzyme which interaction with substrate.
- **Zymogen:** is the active form of enzyme.

## Factors effecting enzyme activity

1. **Enzyme concentration:** If the amount of enzyme is increased by two fold, the reaction rate is doubled.
2. **Substrate concentration:** The rate of reaction is directly proportional to the substrate available.
3. **Temperature:** The rate of enzyme may increase with increasing in temperature but up to a certain limit. (37C is optimum temperature).
4. **Value of pH**
