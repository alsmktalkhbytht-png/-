What Are Viruses
Viruses are distinct from living organisms.
Viruses are living form (not living organisms), submicroscopic,
obligate intracellular parasites; they can only be seen with a
special, very powerful microscope called an "electron microscope,"
Viruses differ from other microorganisms in a number of
characteristics:
1. They have no cellular structure, consisting only of proteins and
nucleic acid (DNA or RNA).
2. They have no metabolic systems of their own, but rather
depend on the synthetic mechanism of a living host cell. Viruses
exploit normal cellular metabolism by delivering their own
genetic information (DNA or RNA).
3. Viruses infect other organism: bacteria (so-called
bacteriophages), protozoa, algae, fungi, plants, animals, and
humans.
4. Virus definition Viruses are particles produced from the
assembly of pre-formed components produced by infected cell.
5. Virus particles (virions) themselves do not grow or undergo
division. Viruses lack the genetic information which encodes
apparatus necessary for the generation of metabolic energy or for
protein synthesis (ribosomes).

Composition of virus particle
Virus particle composed of a genome which is either DNA or RNA
encased by a protein coat called Capsid. This type of virus is called
Naked virus.
Some viruses have in addition to capsid an envelope which
composed of lipid taken from infected cell and some virus proteins.
The virus that has envelope is called Enveloped virus.

Direct Examination of Viruses
Direct viral examination using light and electron microscopy is a
crucial tool in medical virology. It allows for the direct visualization
of viruses, providing valuable insights into their morphology,
presence, and potential role in disease.
3

Light Microscopy:
While viruses are generally too small to be seen with a standard light
microscope, certain viral infections can induce characteristic
changes in infected cells that can be observed. These changes
include the formation of:
1. Inclusion bodies: These are aggregates of viral proteins or
nucleic acids within the nucleus or cytoplasm of infected cells. Their
presence and appearance can be indicative of specific viral
infections.

4

2. Cytopathogenic effects (CPE): These are morphological
changes in infected cells, such as rounding, swelling, or the
formation of syncytia (fused cells). CPE can be observed in cell
cultures infected with certain viruses.

Electron Microscopy:
Electron microscopy (EM) offers significantly higher resolution
than light microscopy, allowing for the direct visualization of
viruses. Two main types of EM are used in virology:

1. Transmission electron microscopy (TEM): In TEM, a beam of
electrons is transmitted through a thin section of the sample. This
technique is useful for examining the internal structure of viruses
and infected cells.

Applications of Direct Viral Examination:
1. Diagnosis of viral infections: Direct examination can help identify
the presence of viruses in clinical specimens, such as respiratory
secretions, stool, or blood.
7

2. Characterization of new viruses: EM can be used to visualize the
morphology of newly discovered viruses, aiding in their
classification and characterization.
3. Monitoring viral replication: Direct examination can be used to
study the replication cycle of viruses in infected cells.
4. Evaluation of antiviral therapies: Direct examination can be used
to assess the efficacy of antiviral drugs in inhibiting viral replication.
Limitations:
1. Sensitivity: Direct examination may not be sensitive enough to
detect low levels of virus.
2. Specificity: The morphology of some viruses may be similar,
making it difficult to distinguish between closely related viruses.
3. Technical expertise: Both light and electron microscopy require
specialized equipment and trained personnel.
Despite these limitations, direct viral examination remains an
important tool in medical virology, providing valuable insights into
the nature and behavior of viruses. It is often used in conjunction
with other diagnostic techniques, such as nucleic acid amplification
tests (NAATs) and serological tests, to provide a comprehensive
picture of viral infections.
