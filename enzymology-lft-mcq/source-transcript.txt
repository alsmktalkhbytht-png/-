Liver Function Tests (LFT)  [student notes: Abdullah Khalid]

Liver function tests measure various chemicals in the blood made by the liver. Abnormal result indicates a problem with the liver and may help to identify the cause.

Major function of liver:
1. Bile production and excretion.
2. Excretion of bilirubin, cholesterol, hormones, and drugs.
3. Metabolism of fats, proteins, and carbohydrates.
4. Enzyme activation.
5. Storage of glycogen, vitamins, and minerals.
6. Synthesis of plasma proteins, such as albumin, and clotting factors.

The main tests for liver function are:
1. Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) also called Glutamic-Pyruvic Transaminase (GPT).
2. Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST) also called Glutamic-Oxaloacetic Transaminase (GOT).
3. Serum Billirubin (Total, Direct and indirect)
4. Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)
5. Albumin, Total protein and etc.

Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) / Glutamic-Pyruvic Transaminase (GPT):
ALT enzyme catalyzes (reversible transamination reaction) the transfer of an amino group from L-alanine to a-ketoglutarate to produce pyruvate and L-glutamate.
[Figure: alanine amino transferase — pyruvate/alanine, glutamate/alpha-ketoglutarate]
This test is primarily used to diagnose liver disease and to monitor the course of treatment for hepatitis, active post necrotic cirrhosis, and the effects of later drug therapy.
Its an enzyme found primarily in the liver and kidney. Normally low of ALT exits in the serum, this enzyme level increased with liver damage and used for screening and\or monitor liver disease.
ALT is found in plasma and in various body tissues but is most commonly associated with the liver. High concentrations occur in the liver, and relatively low concentrations are found in the heart, muscle, and kidney.
- ALT is more specific than AST for liver disease, but the AST is more sensitive to alcoholic liver disease.
- ALT also differentiates between hemolytic jaundice and jaundice due to liver disease.

Reference Values:
Adults:   Males: 10–40 U/L ; Females: 7–35 U/L
Newborns: 13–45 U/L

Clinical significance of ALT: Increased ALT levels are found in the following conditions:
1. Hepatocellular disease
2. Alcoholic cirrhosis
3. Obstructive jaundice or biliary obstruction
4. Viral, infectious, or toxic hepatitis
5. Myocardial infarction, heart failure
6. Severe burns
7. Obesity

Principle (ALT): Kinetic determination of Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT) according to the following reaction.
L-Alanine + alpha-ketoglutarate --ALT--> Pyruvate + L-Glutamate
Pyruvate + NADH + H+ --LDH--> L-Lactate + NAD+   (LDH = indicator)
ALT = Alanine aminotransferase ; LDH = Lactate dehydrogenase
Procedure: Work reagent 1000 uL ; Sample 100 uL. Mix and incubate for 1 min at 37 [°C] then read the change in absorbance per 20 sec during 1 min. [× 340 nm]
CALCULATION: SGPT activity (U/L) = ΔA / min × 1745
[student notes: required. pyruvate is directly proportional to the amount and activity of ALT; the second (indicator) reaction is used because measuring pyruvate is difficult — NADH is consumed and converted to NAD.]

Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST) / Glutamic-Oxaloacetic Transaminase (GOT):
Its an enzyme that catalyzes the reversible reaction of conversion of aspartate and a-ketoglutarate to form oxaloacetate and glutamate (Aspartate donates its amino group to form oxaloacetate, a-Ketoglutarate accepts the amino group to form glutamate).
This reaction require the coenzyme pyridoxine phosphate (a derivative of vitamin B6).
[Figure: aspartate + alpha-ketoglutarate <=> oxaloacetate + glutamate ; Aminotransferase (Transaminase)]
GOT is an enzyme present in tissues of high metabolic activity; decreasing concentrations of GOT are found in the heart, liver, skeletal muscle, kidney, brain, pancreas, spleen, and lungs.
The enzyme is released into the circulation following the injury or death of cells. Any disease that causes change in these highly metabolic tissues will result in a rise in GOT levels.
- This test is used to evaluate liver and heart disease. GOT is usually ordered along with the GPT.
- This enzyme has two names, Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST) and Glutamic-Oxaloacetic Transaminase (GOT).

Reference Values: 5–45 U/L

Clinical significance: Increased AST levels occur in:
1. Myocardial infarction (MI).
2. Liver diseases (Acute hepatitis, chronic hepatitis, Alcoholic hepatitis, etc)
3. Hypothyroidism.
4. Trauma and irradiation of skeletal muscle.
5. Hemolytic anemia, exhaustion, heat stroke and etc.

Principle (AST): Kinetic determination of Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST) based upon the following reaction.
L-Aspartate + alpha-ketoglutarate --AST--> Oxaloacetate + L-Glutamate
Oxaloacetate + NADH + H+ --MDH--> L-Malate + NAD+
AST = Aspartate aminotransferase ; MDH = Malate dehydrogenase
Procedure: Work reagent 1000 uL ; Sample 100 uL. Mix and incubate for 1 min at 37 then read the change in absorbance per min during 3 min.
CALCULATION: SGOT activity (U/L) = ΔA / min × 1745
