Principles of Research — Dr. Linda Munjad Hadi — (the professor's own MCQ file; correct answer highlighted/bold in the source)

1. What is definition of the scientific method:
   A. A process that relies primarily on expert opinion
   B. A fixed set of rules used only in laboratory experiments
   C. A method for confirming existing beliefs through selected observations
   D. A statistical technique used to prove that a hypothesis is true
   E. A systematic, evidence-based approach to investigate questions and develop conclusions from measurable data [correct]

2. Which feature means that scientific conclusions depend on collected observations or measurements:
   A. Reproducible
   B. Objective
   C. Empirical [correct]
   D. Transparent
   E. Self-correcting

3. Why is a scientific approach useful when investigating a laboratory problem:
   A. It makes results independent of measurement procedures
   B. It removes the need to define variables
   C. It guarantees that every result will be statistically significant
   D. It helps define questions and procedures so evidence and conclusions can be evaluated by others [correct]
   E. It prevents new evidence from changing earlier explanations

4. Which characteristic of scientific investigation aims to minimize personal bias:
   A. Empirical
   B. Objective [correct]
   C. Testable
   D. Controlled
   E. Reproducible

5. Which sequence best represents the major steps of the scientific method:
   A. Observation/problem, research question, background research, hypothesis, design, data collection, analysis, conclusion, communication [correct]
   B. Hypothesis, conclusion, observation, communication, data collection
   C. Research question, data collection, hypothesis, observation, conclusion, study design
   D. Study design, communication, observation, background research, analysis, hypothesis
   E. Observation, statistical test, publication, hypothesis, research question

6. A laboratory finds that delayed plasma samples have lower glucose readings. What is the observation:
   A. A defined issue requiring investigation
   B. The effect of delay is uncertain
   C. A directly recorded finding that delayed samples give lower glucose readings [correct]
   D. The hypothesis that delay decreases glucose
   E. The conclusion that all glucose samples must be processed immediately

7. Which research question is most clearly measurable and focused:
   A. What happens to samples over time?
   B. Why is glucose important?
   C. Do laboratory delays cause problems?
   D. Does a 2-hour delay before plasma separation change measured glucose concentration? [correct]
   E. Are delayed samples worse than other samples?

8. What is a key purpose of conducting a literature review before a study:
   A. To guarantee the study will confirm its hypothesis
   B. To avoid unnecessary duplication and identify gaps or limitations in prior evidence [correct]
   C. To replace the need for an experimental design
   D. To select only sources that support the expected result
   E. To determine the final conclusion before collecting data

9. Which statement best describes a hypothesis:
   A. A statement that cannot be changed by new evidence
   B. A broad topic area chosen for investigation
   C. A summary of the results after statistical analysis
   D. A procedure for controlling laboratory quality
   E. A specific, testable statement about an expected relationship or difference between variables [correct]

10. In a study of processing delay and glucose concentration, which is the independent variable:
   A. Processing delay [correct]

11. In the same study, which is the dependent variable:
   A. Tube type
   B. Processing delay
   C. Glucose concentration [correct]
   D. Temperature
   E. Sample identifier

12. How does a study design relate to the scientific method:
   A. The scientific method guides the investigation, while study design specifies how evidence will be obtained [correct]
   B. The study design is the broad framework for reasoning, while the scientific method is the specific evidence collection plan
   C. They are identical terms for statistical analysis
   D. Study design is used only after a conclusion is reached
   E. The scientific method applies only to experimental studies

13. Which practice supports reliable data collection:
   A. Use different procedures for each sample to explore variation
   B. Use standardized procedures, defined units, and record missing data or protocol deviations [correct]
   C. Record only results that match the hypothesis
   D. Avoid coded identifiers so samples are easier to recognize
   E. Skip calibration if the analyzer has recently been serviced

14. What is the difference between data analysis and interpretation:
   A. Analysis proves whether a hypothesis is true
   B. Analysis assesses clinical relevance; interpretation calculates raw measurements only
   C. Analysis and interpretation are unrelated steps
   D. Interpretation must occur before data are collected
   E. Analysis organises and summarises data; interpretation relates findings to the question and considers relevance and limitations [correct]

15. Which conclusion is scientifically appropriate:
   A. A conclusion that states the hypothesis was proven
   B. A conclusion that generalizes to every population regardless of the study sample
   C. A conclusion that omits alternative explanations
   D. A conclusion that matches the strength and scope of the evidence and reports important limitations [correct]
   E. A conclusion based only on expected results

16. Why should researchers report unexpected or negative findings:
   A. They make further analysis unnecessary
   B. They should be reported only when they support the hypothesis
   C. They help provide a complete, transparent record that others can evaluate and reproduce [correct]
   D. They prove that the study design was invalid
   E. They are relevant only to commercial studies

17. Which statement about good study design and statistical analysis is correct:

   B. Statistical analysis can repair every design problem
   C. Confounding is irrelevant when sample size is large

18. In an ELISA wash-step study, what should be evaluated:

   D. Fabricate missing values to complete the dataset
