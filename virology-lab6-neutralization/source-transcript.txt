Virus Neutralization Test (VNT)


Neutralization of viruses by antibodies is a phenomenon of
fundamental medical and biological importance. Importance in
virology (e.g., measuring protective immunity, vaccine efficacy, and
virus identification)
1. Antibodies can neutralize (decrease the replication of) viruses by
blocking attachment to the host cell, preventing penetration of the
host cell membrane, or prevent with uncoating of the virus within
the cell.
2. Neutralizing antibodies typically recognize proteins or
glycoproteins on the virion surface.
3. Some antibodies that bind to virion surface proteins or
glycoproteins are not neutralizing. In some cases, such antibodies
may contribute to immunity, whereas in others, they may enhance
infection.
4. The magnitude of neutralization mediated by a given antibody
may vary with the host cell used for the measurement.
5. Neutralization in vitro is usually related to protection in vivo, but
these two properties are not always perfectly correlated.


Methodology principles
The VNT involves mixing serial dilutions of the serum sample with
a known amount of virus.

                                   1
The mixture is then incubated with susceptible cells.
If neutralizing antibodies are present, they will bind to the virus and
prevent it from infecting the cells.
The highest dilution of serum that completely inhibits viral infection
is considered the neutralization titer.


Methods
1. Sample Preparation: The sample, usually blood serum, is diluted
to create a series of dilutions. This is done to determine the highest
dilution at which antibodies can still neutralize the virus
2. Virus Incubation: A known quantity of the virus is mixed with
each dilution of the sample. This mixture is incubated, allowing any
neutralizing antibodies in the sample to bind to the virus.
3. Cell Culture: Cells susceptible to the virus are added to the virus-
antibody mixture. If neutralizing antibodies are present, they will
prevent the virus from infecting the cells.
4. Observation: The cell culture is monitored for signs of viral
infection. If the cells remain healthy, it indicates that the antibodies
in the sample neutralized the virus at that dilution.
5. Titer Determination: The highest dilution of the sample that still
prevents viral infection is the antibody titer. This titer indicates the
level of neutralizing antibodies present in the sample.


The VNT is used in a variety of applications, including:
1. Vaccine development: To assess the efficacy of vaccines by
measuring the levels of neutralizing antibodies induced by
vaccination.

                                   2
2. Diagnosis of viral infections: To detect the presence of
neutralizing antibodies in patients suspected of having a viral
infection.
3. Epidemiological studies: To determine the prevalence of
neutralizing antibodies in a population.

Interpretation of Results
The results of the VNT are typically expressed as a titer, which is
the highest dilution of serum that completely inhibits viral infection.
A higher titer indicates a higher level of neutralizing antibodies.
Limitations
The VNT is a complex and time-consuming assay. It requires
specialized equipment and expertise. It can also be difficult to
standardize the assay across different laboratories.
