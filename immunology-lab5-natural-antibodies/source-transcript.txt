Natural Antibodies Study
Natural Antibodies (NAbs) are most commonly defined as immunoglobulins
present in the absence of exogenous antigen stimulation. They are produced
without any previous infection, vaccination, other foreign antigen exposure or
passive immunization, contrasted to regular and irregular antibodies.
Generation of diverse class of antibodies starts during organism development
even before birth. These NAbs providing a first line of defense against infection
thereby, allowing time for a specific antibody response to be mounted. In
addition, NAbs provide immediate protection against infection while the
adaptive immune system provides a specific and long-lasting response.
Characteristics of Nabs
NAbs have unique characteristics that also contribute to their functional roles
and set them apart from antigen-specific antibodies. Such characteristics
include germline structure and a restricted repertoire. Functionally antibodies
are not restricted to protect host from invading pathogens albeit they are key
regulator of waste management in system generated through cell lysis.
Natural antibodies are essentially antibodies of the immunoglobulin M (IgM)
isotype present in the circulation of normal humans and other mammalian
species. They are detectable in the serum of healthy individuals before
deliberate immunization. They are often directed against highly conserved
epitopes and often bind to ligands of varying chemical composition with low
affinity. This antibody subset contrasts with immune antibodies, which are
produced in response to the introduction of antigens to the immune system.
Natural antibodies are frequently directed to intracellular structures, rather
than to cell‐surface antigens. They have been found to play an innate‐like role
in protection against infectious agents and to exert homeostatic functions in a
variety of experimental models. Other investigations suggest that natural
antibodies can play a pathogenic role in autoinflammatory diseases. In case of
autoimmune disease these natural antibodies functions deregulate that
hampers the proper functioning of immune machinery either by hyper immune
responses or by tissue degeneration.
NAb-producing cells and their History
Determining and subsequently examining the B cells producing NAbs have been
began since the early 1980s despite NAbs being studied since the late 1960s.
NAb producing B-1a cells were first identified in mice and characterized by
surface expression of CD5+, IgMhigh, IgDlow, CD19high, B220low, CD23−, and
CD43+, which contrasts with the surface phenotype of follicular B-2 cells: CD5−,
IgMlow, IgDhigh, CD19+, B220+, CD23+, and CD43−. Studies have demonstrated
that B-1a cells are found in the peritoneal cavity, pleural cavity, spleen, bone
marrow, lymph nodes, and blood of mice. Furthermore, various subsets of B-1a
cells have been identified. Throughout the many years of B-1a cell investigation,
it has been shown that not all subsets of murine B-1a cells secrete NAbs. More
recently, focus has been given in determining the subset of B cells in humans
capable of producing NAbs. Early studies in humans focused on CD5+ peripheral
B cells. Further investigation is still required in the human system to determine
whether other subsets of NAb producing B cells exist and the location of such
subsets beyond peripheral blood.
Many aspects of NAbs and the cells generating them have yet to be studied in
great detail:
• the reactivity and function of NAbs in health and different diseases.
• the behavior of the NAb repertoire with increasing age.
• the regulation of NAb production and auto-reactivity.
The functions of natural antibodies
Beyond immediate protection from infection, NAbs have been shown to play
various functional roles in the immune system, which include:
1. Clearance of apoptotic debris.
2. Suppression of allergic responses.
3. Regulation of B cell responses.
4. Selection of the B cell repertoire.
5. Protection from cancer.
6. Regulation of B cell development.
7. Protection against atherosclerosis.
8. Protection from bacteria, viruses and fungi.
*These various functions of NAbs are afforded by their broad reactivity.
