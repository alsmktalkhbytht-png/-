MCQs Lecture 5 — Classification of Researches
Research Methods — Dr. Fayyad (the professor's own end-of-lecture MCQs; correct answer in bold in the source)

1. What is the primary purpose of basic (fundamental) research:
   A. To increase understanding of fundamental principles and phenomena [correct]
   B. To immediately launch a commercial product
   C. To select a policy for a specific organization
   D. To provide routine patient care
   E. To market an established treatment

2. Which statement best describes applied research:
   A. It avoids practical problems
   B. It focuses only on theoretical explanations
   C. It addresses a specific practical problem using knowledge and methods [correct]
   D. It is limited to historical records
   E. It never uses established theories

3. Which is a typical outcome of basic research:
   A. A refined theory or new scientific understanding [correct]
   B. A guaranteed clinical cure
   C. A new hospital billing policy
   D. A finalized consumer product
   E. A routine diagnostic service

4. Which example is basic biological research:
   A. Comparing two approved cancer therapies in patients
   B. Designing a test to detect infection earlier
   C. Creating a tissue repair scaffold
   D. Investigating how immune cells recognize pathogens [correct]
   E. Evaluating a vaccine for use in a population

5. Testing compounds that may treat an infection is an example of:
   A. Basic research
   B. Qualitative research
   C. Descriptive research only
   D. Case study research only
   E. Applied research [correct]

6. A project studying how genes function, without an immediate treatment goal, is primarily:
   A. Applied research
   B. Experimental research by definition
   C. Basic research [correct]
   D. Clinical research
   E. Descriptive research

7. What kind of data does quantitative research primarily use:
   A. Personal narratives
   B. Numerical measurements [correct]
   C. Only archival documents
   D. Unstructured observations only
   E. Images without measurements

8. Which method is commonly associated with qualitative research:
   A. Statistical tests on numerical variables
   B. Calculating averages only
   C. Randomized drug dosing only
   D. Interviews and observations [correct]
   E. Laboratory calibration

9. A researcher surveys 500 patients and then interviews 20 of them to understand their experiences. This is:
   A. Observational-only research
   B. Mixed-methods research [correct]
   C. Basic research only
   D. Case study research only
   E. Experimental research

10. When is exploratory research especially useful:
   A. When a topic is not yet clearly understood [correct]
   B. When the sole aim is to prove a known result
   C. When all variables are already fully measured
   D. When describing an established population precisely
   E. Only after a clinical treatment is approved

11. The main aim of descriptive research is to:
   A. Explain the cause of every observed pattern
   B. Describe characteristics or conditions accurately [correct]
   C. Manipulate variables under controlled conditions
   D. Combine interviews and numerical data
   E. Develop a practical product

12. Which question is most closely linked to descriptive research:
   A. Why does the condition occur?
   B. Which intervention should be marketed?
   C. What mechanism causes the effect?
   D. What are the characteristics of the affected group? [correct]
   E. How can a theory be disproved?

13. Explanatory research primarily seeks to:
   A. List the features of a population
   B. Collect only open-ended comments
   C. Understand relationships and explain why or how a phenomenon occurs [correct]
   D. Identify a new species
   E. Summarize numbers without interpretation

14. What defines experimental research:
   A. The researcher deliberately manipulates one or more variables and observes effects [correct]
   B. The researcher only records events without intervention
   C. The study focuses on one bounded case
   D. The researcher uses interviews exclusively
   E. The study describes a group at one point in time

15. In observational research, the researcher:
   A. Assigns treatments to all participants
   B. Manipulates the principal exposure
   C. Systematically records phenomena without directly manipulating variables [correct]
   D. Must use focus groups
   E. Must combine numerical and descriptive data

16. Which design is especially suited to an in-depth investigation of one defined case:
   A. Experimental study
   B. Descriptive
   C. Quantitative survey
   D. Exploratory trial
   E. Case study [correct]

17. A single research project can belong to multiple classifications because:
   A. Each project must use only one method
   B. Classifications depend on different features such as purpose, approach, design, and data [correct]
   C. Research categories are interchangeable
   D. The same project changes its results over time
   E. Only the source of data matters

18. A study using numerical blood pressure readings and statistical comparisons is most clearly:
   A. Qualitative
   B. Exploratory only
   C. Case study
   D. Quantitative [correct]
   E. Basic by definition

19. A study using focus groups to explore patients’ perceptions is most clearly:
   A. Quantitative
   B. Experimental
   C. Qualitative [correct]
   D. Applied by definition
   E. Longitudinal

20. A controlled study comparing vaccine doses to assess immune response is most likely:
   A. Experimental research [correct]
   B. Case study research
   C. Qualitative research
   D. Descriptive research only
   E. Basic research by definition

21. Developing a diagnostic test that detects disease earlier is an example of:
   A. Applied research [correct]
   B. Basic research
   C. Case study research
   D. Exploratory research
   E. Observational research

22. Which statement about applied research is accurate:
   A. It is conducted only in medicine
   B. It can improve products, processes, policies, services, or practices [correct]
   C. It cannot use established knowledge
   D. It aims only to create new theories
   E. It does not relate to real-world needs

23. Investigating how neural circuits support memory, without an immediate goal, is an example of:
   A. Applied research
   B. Clinical research
   C. Basic research [correct]
   D. Descriptive research only
   E. Mixed-methods research

24. A researcher observes animal behavior in its natural setting and records it without changing conditions. This is:
   A. Experimental research
   B. Observational research [correct]
   C. Applied research
   D. Mixed-methods research
   E. Case study research by definition
