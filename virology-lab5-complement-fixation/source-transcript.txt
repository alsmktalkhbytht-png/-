Complement Fixation Test
The complement fixation test (CFT) is a serological test used to detect the
presence of specific antibodies or antigens in a patient's serum. It is based on
the principle of complement fixation, where complement proteins are
consumed in an antigen-antibody reaction. This test has been widely used in
the diagnosis of various viral infections, particularly those where viral
culture is difficult or unavailable.
Complement is a protein (Globulin) present in the normal serum and found
in many types. When Ag is complexed with Ab on the surface of cell,
complement causes lysis of cell.
The CFT has been used in the diagnosis of various viral infections,
including: Influenza, Measles, Mumps, Rubella, Herpes simplex virus,
Cytomegalovirus.
Procedure:
1. Serum Preparation:
o

Heat inactivate the patient’s serum at 56°C for 30 minutes to
destroy endogenous complement.

2. Primary Incubation:
o
o
o

Mix patient serum with known viral antigen.
Add a measured amount of complement.
Incubate the mixture at 37°C for 30 minutes to allow antigen-antibody interaction and complement fixation.

3. Indicator System Addition:
o
o

Add sheep RBCs (SRBCs) sensitized with hemolysin.
Incubate at 37°C for another 30 minutes.

Positive Test: No hemolysis observed, indicating complement
fixation due to antigen-antibody complex formation.
Negative Test: Hemolysis observed, indicating free
complement, hence no antigen-antibody reaction. See figure
above.

Advantages of CFT
1. Ability to screen against a large number of viral and bacterial infections at
the same time.
2. Economical.
Disadvantages of CFT
1. Not sensitive - cannot be used for immunity screening
2. Time consuming and labor intensive
3. Often non-specific e.g. cross-reactivity
