Research Methods
Lecture Four
Research process

Research process consists of series of actions or steps necessary to
effectively carry out research. These actions or steps are;
  1. Formulating the Research Problem
  2. Extensive Literature Survey
  3. Developing the Research Hypothesis
  4. Preparing the Research Design
  5. Determining the Research Design
  6. Collecting the Research Data
  7. Execution of the Project
  8. Analysis of Data
  9. Hypothesis Testing
  10. Generalization and Interpretation
  11. Preparing of the Report or Presentation of the Result

  1. Formulating the Research Problem
  A research problem is a specific issue within the field that you
  can investigate. Examples:

  Antibiotic resistance patterns: Local bacterial isolates may
  show changing resistance patterns. A study could describe
  resistance findings in approved laboratory records over a
  defined period
  Urine specimen handling: Delays between collection and
  processing may affect some urine test findings. A study could
  examine whether processing time is associated with selected
  results.

  2. Extensive Literature Survey:

                                 1
  Once the problem is formulated the researcher should undertake
extensive literature survey connected with the problem.
Literature of interest could be obtained from: Academic journals;
Conference proceedings; Scientific reports and Books, depending
on the nature of the research problem.
   3. Development of Working Hypothesis
After extensive literature survey, researcher should state in clear
terms the working hypothesis or hypotheses.
Working hypothesis is tentative assumption made in order to draw
out and test its logical or empirical consequences.
It’s very important and provides the focal point for research.
Example:
   - Role of minerals on growth of the bacterium Pseudomonas
      aeruginosa.
   - Effect of fructose on blood sugar.


  4. Preparing the Research Design

  Preparing a research design means creating a clear plan for how
  you will answer your research question.

  a. First, choose the appropriate approach—quantitative,
     qualitative, or mixed methods.
  b. Then decide what samples, records, or participants you will
     study; how you will collect and analyze the data; and how you
     will protect privacy, safety, and research quality.

  Example: To investigate common reasons for specimen
  rejection, you could review approved laboratory records from a
  defined period, categorize the rejection reasons, and summarize
  them using counts and percentages.

  5. Determining Experimental Design


                                 2
Determining the experimental design means deciding how to test a
research question under controlled conditions. The researcher
identifies the groups or conditions to compare, the outcome to
measure, and how to reduce bias and keep procedures consistent.

Example: To compare two laboratory testing methods, use the same
suitable samples with both methods, follow consistent procedures,
and compare the results. Any required approvals and laboratory
safety procedures must be followed.

1. Key Principles of Experimental Design
To ensure rigorous and reliable results, a robust experimental
design relies on four core principles:

a. Control: Keeping all extraneous variables constant (or
accounting for them) so that any change in the dependent variable
can be confidently attributed to the manipulation of the
independent variable.
b. Randomization: Randomly assigning participants or subjects to
   experimental and control groups to eliminate selection bias and
   distribute confounding variables evenly.

c. Replication: Repeating the experiment across multiple subjects
   or trials to ensure that findings are reliable, reproducible, and
   not due to random chance.


d. Grouping: Grouping similar experimental units (e.g., by age,
   skill level, or baseline health) to reduce the impact of known
   confounding variables.

2. Pilot and refine the plan.
Try the procedure on a small scale to find practical problems
before the main study.

6. Collecting the Research Data:
                                 3
a. There are several ways of collecting the appropriate data which
differ considerably in context of cost, time and other resources at
the disposal of the researcher.
b. Primary data can be collected either through experiment or
through survey.

7. Execution of the Project:
Execution of project is a very important step in the research
process.
a. If the execution of the project proceeds on correct lines, the
data to be collected would be adequate and dependable.
b. A careful watch should be kept for unanticipated
factors in order to keep the survey realistic as much as possible.

8. Analysis of Data
a. The analysis of data requires a number of closely related
operations such as establishment of categories, the application of
these categories to raw data through coding, tabulation and then
drawing statistical inference.
b. In the process of analysis, relationships of differences
supporting or conflicting with original or new hypothesis should
be subjected to tests of significance to determine with what
validity data can be said to indicate any conclusions.

9. Hypothesis Testing:
a. After analyzing the data, the researcher is in a position to test
the hypothesis, if any, had formulated earlier.
Do the facts support the hypothesis or they happen to be
contrary?
b. Hypothesis testing will result in either accepting the hypothesis
or in rejecting it.
c If the researcher had no hypothesis to start with,
generalizations established on the basis of data may be stated.


                                  4
10. Generalizations and Interpretation:
• If a hypothesis is tested and upheld several times, it
may be possible for the researcher to arrive at generalization i.e.
to build a theory.
• As a matter of fact, the real value of research lies in its ability to
arrive at certain generalizations.
• If the researcher had no hypothesis to start with, he
might seek to explain his findings on the basis of some theory. It is
known as interpretation.

11. Preparation of the Report (Paper) or the Thesis:
a. Finally, the researcher has to prepare the report of what has been
done by him/her.
b. The preliminary pages carry title, acknowledgements and
forward and then index.
The main text of the report should have Title, Name of author(s),
Adress, Abstract, Introduction, Review of literature,
Methodology (Materials and Methods), Results, Discussion and
References.
