                                                                                              Insulin
A peptide hormone produced by the beta cells of the pancreas that regulates glucose
metabolism.
Plays a key role in maintaining blood sugar levels.
Why Estimate Insulin?
 o Important in diagnosing diabetes mellitus (Type 1 and Type 2) and other metabolic
disorders.
o Helps in assessing insulin resistance, hyperinsulinemia, and pancreatic function.

   Types of Insulin Estimation Methods
  1. Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay [ELISA].
  2. High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC).
  3. Mass Spectrometry.

  Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA)
  Principle:
  An antigen-antibody reaction is coupled with an enzyme-catalyzed color change reaction
  to detect insulin concentration. Procedure:
  1. Coat a microplate with insulin-specific antibodies.
  2. Add serum samples; insulin will bind to the antibody.
   3. Add an enzyme-conjugated secondary antibody specific to insulin
  4. Add substrate that the enzyme converts into a colored product.
   5. Measure the color change using a spectrophotometer.
   • Advantages: No radiation, safer, widely available.
• Disadvantages: Moderate sensitivity compared to RIA.

Normal range
1. Fasting Insulin Levels
   Normal Range: 2.6 - 24.9 μU/mL (18 - 174 pmol/L).
2. Insulin Resistance and Hyperinsulinemia
Elevated Insulin Levels:
Range: > 24.9 μU/mL (>174 pmol/L)
3. Low Insulin Levels
Range: < 2.6 μU/mL, (<18 pmol/L)

Estimating Insulin using ELISA
Materials Needed:
 Insulin ELISA kit (pre-packaged)
 Microplate reader
 Serum samples (patient samples or standard insulin solution)
Distilled water, pipettes, and tips
Steps:
1. Preparation: Prepare reagents as per the kit instructions.
2. Coating the Plate: Add insulin-specific antibodies to the wells.
3. Sample Addition: Add patient serum or insulin standard solutions to the wells.
4. Incubation: Allow the reaction to occur, typically 1 hour at room temperature.
5. Washing: Remove unbound substances using a wash buffer. 6. Enzyme Conjugate
Addition: Add the enzyme-labeled secondary antibody.
7. Substrate Addition: Add the substrate to produce a color change.
8. Measurement: Measure the optical density using a microplate reader at a specific
wavelength (usually 450 nm).
9. Calculation: Use a standard curve to calculate the insulin concentration.

Quality Control and Considerations
 1. Calibration: Use standard insulin solutions to generate a standard curve.
 2. Sensitivity: Check the lowest detectable concentration of insulin.
 3. Accuracy and Precision: Perform replicate tests to ensure reliable results
