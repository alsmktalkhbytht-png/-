What is CIE?
CIE is a rapid immunological test.
It detects a specific antigen or antibody.
The test uses an electric current to move antigen and antibody toward each other.
A visible precipitation line means a reaction occurred.

Principle of CIE
Antigen and antibody are placed in separate wells in an agarose gel.
An electric field is applied.
They move toward each other.
When they meet at the right proportion, a precipitin line forms.

=== 7  imgs=['image5.png']
Gel Setup
Two small wells are made in the agarose gel.
One well receives the antigen.
The other well receives the antibody.
The wells are placed close enough for the reaction to occur.
6
=== 8  imgs=['image7.png', 'image6.png']
Practical Procedure
1. Prepare the agarose gel.
2. Make two wells in the gel.
3. Add antigen to one well and antibody to the other.
4. Place the gel in the electrophoresis chamber.
5. Apply the electric current for the required time.
6. Observe the gel for a precipitation line.

Positive Result
A visible precipitin line appears between the wells.
This means the antigen reacted with its specific antibody.
8
**Examples of precipitin lines**
Negative Result
No precipitin line appears between the wells.
This means no visible antigen–antibody precipitation was detected.
=== 10  imgs=['image10.png']
Why Does a Line Form?
Antigen binds to its specific antibody.
Many antigen–antibody complexes join together.
The complexes become large enough to be seen as a white line.

=== 12  imgs=[]
Advantages
Simple technique
Faster than passive diffusion methods
Low-cost equipment
Visible result
Useful for teaching antigen–antibody reactions
12
=== 13  imgs=[]
Limitations
Less sensitive than many modern immunoassays
Usually gives a qualitative result
Needs an electrophoresis setup
Weak lines may be difficult to see
Correct test conditions are important
13
=== 14  imgs=[]
Summary
CIE combines immunology with electrophoresis.
Antigen and antibody move toward each other in agarose gel.
A precipitin line indicates a positive reaction.
The method is simple, fast, and mainly qualitative.
