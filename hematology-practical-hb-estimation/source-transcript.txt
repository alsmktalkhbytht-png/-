Estimation of Hemoglobin with Cyanmethemoglobin method (Drabkin's method)
Blood is diluted in Drabkin's solution which consists of potassium cyanide and potassium ferricyanide.
Drabkin's Reagent is used for the quantitative, colorimetric determination of hemoglobin concentration in whole blood at 540 nm; this procedure is based on the oxidation of hemoglobin to methemoglobin in the presence of alkaline potassium ferricyanide.
Principle
This method is consider a photometric method and the absorbance of the solution is measured in a spectrophotometer at a 540nm wavelength.
Equipment and reagents
Drabkin's solution which consists of
Potassium cyanide 50 mg
Potassium ferricyanide 200 mg
Potassium dihydrogen phosphate 140 mg
Nonionic detergent 1 ml
distilled water 1 L.
Hb pipette
Spectrophotometer
Procedure
Take 5ml of Drabkin's solution in a test tube.
Collect venous blood. Mix the blood sample by gentle inversion and pour 0.02ml of blood into the previous solution with the help of a Hb pipette .
Mix well and wait for 5min.
Measure the absorbance of this solution at 540nm in a spectrophotometer.
Calculate the hemoglobin concentration using a standard curve.
Result
Standard curve for Hemoglobin estimation by Cyanmethemoglobin method can be prepared by determining several samples of known heamoglobin concentration.
Estimation of hemoglobin by acid hematin method (Sahli’s method)
Principle: hemoglobin in the blood is converted into acid hematin, a brown colored solution formed upon the addition of 1/10 N HCL. The colored solution is then measured colorimetrically by comparing with a standard.
Sahli’s Apparatus
Hemoglobin pipette (0.02 ml or 20 µl capacity)
Sahli’s graduated Hemoglobin tube
Thin glass rod Stirrer for Hemoglobin Tube
Sahli’s Comparator box with brown glass standard
Procedure of sahli’s method
N/10 Hydrochloric acid is taken in Hemoglobin tube (has two graduations – one side gm/dl, and other side shows the Hb %age), up to the mark 20 – the lowest marking (yellow marking).
Venous or Capillary blood is drawn up to 20µl mark of hemoglobin pipette exactly.
For capillary blood draw, boldly prick the tip of the middle or ring finger with the help of Blood lancet or pricking needle. Wipe out the first drop of blood and suck the blood from the second drop in Hb pipette up to the mark of 20 µl. Fill the Hb pipette by capillary action.
Dispense the blood into N/10 hydrochloric acid taken in the hemoglobin tube, rinse the pipette with the same solution and mix properly with the help of stirrer.
Place the tube at room temperature for 10 minutes for complete conversion of hemoglobin into acid hematin.
After the reaction completes, place the Hb tube in the column in Sahli’s Comparator box and start diluting the dark brown coloured compound (Acid Hematin) formed in the Hb tube using the distilled water by adding drop by drop of it into the solution and mix with the help of stirrer after each addition.
This process is done until the endpoint comes matching the color of standard with the color of the test.
Once the color is matched with the standard brown glass, lift the stirrer up and note down the reading in Sahli’s Hb tube by taking the lower meniscus in consideration.
Now add one more drop of distilled water and mix it properly with the help of stirrer. If color is still matching with the standard add another drop till it matches with the standard and note down the reading and, if it gets lighter after adding the first extra drop, it shows reading taken before dilution was correct. Note down that reading as the final result.
Reading of this method is expressed in Hemoglobin gm/dl (gram/100 ml) of blood.
Normal values of hemoglobin
Adult Male: 14-16 gm/dl
Adult Female: 13-15 gm/dl
Newborn: 16-18 gm/dl
The advantages of sahli’s method
Cheap and can be used for hemoglobin estimation where automated hematology analyser is not available. No technical expertise is needed to perform.
The disadvantages of sahli’s method
The color of acid hematin fades with time.
Carboxyhemoglobin, methemoglobin, and sulfhemoglobin are not converted to acid hematin.
Acid hematin solution is not stable and color formation is slow.
Source of light will influence the visual comparison of colors.
Color of brown glass standard fades with time.
Individual variation in matching of color is seen.
