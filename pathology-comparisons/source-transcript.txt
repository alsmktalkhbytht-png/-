III. Acute renal failure (ARF)
 Course: Rapid decline in renal function
 Causes: Many causes, including glomerular disease, principally rapidly progressive GN and acute diffuse proliferative GN
 Features: Rising urea and creatinine, often with reduced urine output *
 Kidney size: Usually normal or enlarged *
IV. Chronic renal failure (CRF)
 Course: Advanced renal impairment progressing over years
 Causes: Chronic glomerulonephritis
 Features: Detected by significant proteinuria, haematuria, hypertension and azotaemia
 Kidney size: Small contracted kidneys

V. Asymptomatic proteinuria
 Definition: Presence of proteinuria unexpectedly in a patient
 Relation to renal disease: May be unrelated to renal disease, or may indicate underlying mild glomerulonephritis
 Examples of non-renal causes: Exercise-induced, extreme lordosis, orthostatic proteinuria
 Age group: Commonly seen in children and adolescents, especially the orthostatic type *
VI. Asymptomatic hematuria
 Definition: Asymptomatic microscopic haematuria
 Relation to renal disease: Has many diverse causes
 Examples of causes: Urinary tract infection, hypercalciuria, IgA nephropathy, Alport syndrome, thin basement membrane disease, renal stones *
 Age group: Common in children and young adolescents

Types of atelectasis:
1. Resorption atelectasis
 Mechanism: Obstruction prevents air from reaching distal airways; trapped air is gradually absorbed, then alveolar collapse follows
 Most common cause: Mucous or mucopurulent plug in a bronchus
 Associated conditions: Postoperative state, bronchial asthma, bronchiectasis, chronic bronchitis, tumor, foreign body aspiration (especially children)
 Area affected: Entire lung, a complete lobe, or one or more segments
2. Compression atelectasis
 Mechanism: Fluid, blood or air accumulates in the pleural cavity and mechanically collapses the adjacent lung
 Common causes: Pleural effusion (most commonly from CHF), pneumothorax, basal atelectasis from elevated diaphragm
 Associated conditions: Bedridden patients, ascites, during and after surgery
 Also called: Passive or relaxation atelectasis
3. Contraction atelectasis
 Mechanism: Local or generalized fibrotic changes in the lung or pleura hamper expansion and increase elastic recoil during expiration
 Cause: Fibrosis
 Also called: Cicatrization atelectasis
 Note: Most serious type, because it causes fibrosis
